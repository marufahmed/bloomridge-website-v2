Most parents start looking for a therapy centre after a difficult week. A teacher has said something, or a relative has, or you watched your child at a birthday party and felt a quiet worry settle in. Then you open the search results and every centre says the same thing: expert team, child friendly, individual attention.

The brochures will not help you tell them apart. The questions you ask on your visit will.

What follows are 9 questions worth asking anywhere in Dhaka, including here at Bloomridge Springs, with our own answers written plainly so you can compare. If a centre cannot answer one, that is information too. Take notes on your phone during the visit, because after 3 centres and a rickshaw ride home they blur together.

## Questions about who does the work

### 1. Who assesses my child, and what happens in that assessment?

Ask who is in the room, how long it takes, and what you are given at the end. Be wary of a place that decides on a plan after 20 minutes of watching your child play, and equally wary of one that hands you a label.

We start with a strengths-first assessment. We map what your child can already do before we look at what is hard, because the things your child can do are the tools we teach with. We do not diagnose. We work with children who have a diagnosis and children who do not, and if a medical question comes up we point you to a doctor.

### 2. Do the therapists and the teachers talk to each other?

This is the question that separates a centre from a collection of rooms. If the occupational therapist works on holding a pencil in one room and the classroom teacher never hears about it, your child has to bridge that gap alone.

Our therapists hand strategies to the teachers daily, and the teachers report back what actually happened in class: what worked at 10 am on a noisy morning, what did not. Both sides work from the same live Student Profile, which records your child's learning style, communication preferences, sensory needs and motivators, and gets updated as we learn more.

### 3. How many children are in a group, and who runs it?

Ask for the number, not the adjective. "Small group" can mean 4 children or 12.

Our group work is a 2-hour session once a week, run by a psychologist, and the content is the skill itself: taking turns, playing with another child, noticing and naming feelings. It is not free play with an adult supervising from a chair.

## Questions about the teaching

### 4. Are my child's goals written down, and can I see them?

If goals live in somebody's head, nobody can tell whether they were met. Ask to see a sample plan with the names removed.

Every child here has an individual education plan, written for a 3-month cycle. Each goal is tied to a specific item in an assessment bank, so it can be scored, and skills are rated on 4 steps: emerging, developing, consistent, independent. You get a copy. You are meant to read it.

### 5. Is there a curriculum, and what does a day actually look like?

A good centre should be able to tell you what is being taught this term, not just what is being worked on in general.

Our school curriculum states the exact letters, words and numbers taught in each trimester, so the teaching does not drift. A typical week is school sessions 4 days a week for 2 hours, 1 group session of 2 hours, and 2 to 4 one-to-one therapy sessions depending on the plan: occupational therapy for fine motor skills, sensory regulation and daily independence, speech and language therapy for early language, clearer speech and asking for what you need, and special education for one-to-one learning at your child's pace.

### 6. What happens when a goal is met?

Ask this one. Some places will keep billing for a skill your child mastered 4 months ago.

When a goal is scored as independent, it comes off the active list and a new goal replaces it in the next cycle, tied to the next step in the curriculum. We would rather tell you your child no longer needs something than keep it running.

## Questions about you, the parent

### 7. How often will I hear about progress, and in what form?

"We will call you if there is a problem" is not a system. Ask for the frequency and the format.

You get a written weekly review. Once a month there is a coaching session for you: what to do at home, what to stop doing, how to build a routine that survives real evenings. You also get take-home worksheets and activity guides matched to your child's level, not generic printouts.

### 8. What does the fee include?

Ask what is inside the monthly fee and what is billed separately: assessments, reports, materials, review meetings, the group session.

Our plans are Foundation, Enhanced and Intensive, and they differ mainly in how many one-to-one therapy sessions a week your child has. The current figures are on the [fees page](/fees/) rather than in a conversation, so you can read them without a person sitting across from you.

## Questions about hard days

### 9. What happens when my child has a bad day?

Every child has them. A child who bites, or lies on the floor and refuses to move, or cries through the whole session. How a centre answers this tells you more than anything on the wall.

Listen for whether they treat behaviour as information or as disobedience. Our answer is that a hard day is data: we look at what came before it, what the room was like, whether your child had a way to say "this is too much" and could not use it. The strategy gets adjusted, the Student Profile gets updated, and you hear about it that week rather than at the end of the term.

One last thing that is not a question. Watch how the staff speak to the children while you stand in the corridor, especially the children not being shown off to visitors. Trust what you see more than what you are told.
